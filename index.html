How does attention learn to suppress salient information?

I am a Ph.D. student in Psychological Sciences at the University of Missouri, where I work with Dr. Nicholas Gaspelin in the Attention & Distraction Lab. I began the Ph.D. program in Fall 2024 after completing my master's degree at Guangzhou University with Dr. Shimin Fu.

During my master's training, my research focused on how salience affects attentional capture and suppression. With support from a competitive scholarship, I conducted a six-month visiting project in Nick's lab, where we examined how task relevance determines whether salience shapes attentional selection.

Since beginning my Ph.D., my work has focused on the mechanisms by which individuals learn to suppress salient information. Using a short-run technique to track rapid learning over just a few exposures, my current research asks how experience can reshape attentional control, with a particular focus on the idea that an initial instance of attentional capture may act as a learning signal that promotes later suppression.

I test these questions using behavioral methods, eye tracking, and event-related potentials. Across this work, I aim to understand how initial capture shapes the attentional control processes that prevent future distraction.
